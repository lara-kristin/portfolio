Reflection

I initially viewed autonomy and specialisation as strengths of agent-based systems. Anne’s point about neglected user constraints and Lana’s concern about rigid roles showed me that autonomy can fail when responsibilities are poorly bounded. This changed my understanding of robustness: preserving constraints and controlling handoffs matters because a requirement lost during planning may never be recovered downstream. In future, I would record user constraints explicitly, approve subquestions before retrieval and allow bounded replanning when weaknesses are detected.

Reflection

I argued that ACLs add meaning beyond method calls. Anne showed I had overstated KQML’s performative limitation, while Dragos shifted my attention to whether receivers have genuine discretion. This changed my view of richer communication: it is only useful when agents can reject, reinterpret or negotiate. Otherwise, ACL semantics add complexity without meaningful communicative autonomy. In future, I would keep deterministic exchanges as typed calls, but record commitments where agents exercise discretion so later behaviour can be checked against them.

Reflection

I explored bias in generative AI, but peer responses challenged both my interpretation of the evidence and the benchmarks used to define fairness. The discussion made me recognise that I had overstated the evidence by treating disparities against benchmarks as proof of amplified training-data bias. It also showed me that fairness targets are value-dependent and that technical choices such as thresholds and missing-data handling can embed judgements. In future projects, I will make evaluation criteria explicit, justify my research agent’s 0.6 relevance threshold and consider whose values those choices reflect.
