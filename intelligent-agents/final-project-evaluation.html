Final Project Evaluation

Evidence and evaluation from the design phase and implemented solution.

Design Phase

I developed the initial design proposal, which the team reviewed and agreed on collaboratively. I wrote Section 4, Design Decisions and Technical Rationale, and created Diagram 2, the UML Sequence Diagram, and Diagram 3, the UML Activity Diagram. I also reviewed my peers’ contributions, provided feedback and integrated the agreed sections into the final report, standardising formatting and references to ensure consistency. We coordinated through WhatsApp and used a shared Google Drive folder to exchange and revise project files.

The design separated planning, retrieval and evaluation into specialised agents coordinated by a deterministic orchestrator, with human review points bounding autonomy. Responsibilities were clear and the workflow traceable. However, routing interactions through the orchestrator constrained the social ability associated with intelligent agents (Wooldridge and Jennings, 1995). This was deliberate: rather than introduce richer agent communication through mechanisms such as KQML, we prioritised deterministic, auditable exchanges because architectures without centralised verification can amplify errors across agent handoffs (Kim et al., 2026). The trade-off improved traceability and error containment but reduced agent-to-agent autonomy. More fundamentally, none of these safeguards had yet been implemented, so bounded autonomy, reliable oversight and error containment remained design claims rather than demonstrated properties.

Implemented Solution

The group design was developed into a working implementation: three agents using Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite for semantic judgement, coordinated by a deterministic orchestrator with two human checkpoints. It retrieves from Semantic Scholar, verifies DOIs against Crossref and produces a saved brief containing selected papers with relevance scores, summaries, themes, gaps and limitations. The workflow is covered by an offline test suite.

The main limitations concern scope and evaluation: the system relies on one literature source and abstracts rather than full texts, has no labelled dataset for measuring retrieval quality, and does not independently verify summary faithfulness. Testing also showed that apparently correct outputs could mask safeguards that were not functioning as intended. A more detailed critical evaluation is provided in the Final Reflective Piece.
